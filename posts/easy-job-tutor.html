Easy-Job-Tutor: An AI Resume Coach for Students

Project

Job hunting for students is opaque. You paste a job description, upload your resume, and wait. What the company is looking for, how your background stacks up against it, what you should work on next — no one tells you.

Easy-Job-Tutor is a small tool I built to change that. It is an AI-powered resume coach aimed at college students and recent graduates, focused on three job tracks that are hard to navigate on your own: AI product, AI agent development, and AI operations.

How it works

The flow is deliberately simple. You paste a job description and upload your resume — PDF, Word, or plain text. The tool reads the JD, identifies the target role, and scores your resume against it across four dimensions: core skills, project experience, education, and overall fit. A score ring shows the result at a glance, and a gap list tells you, in order of importance, what the JD wants that your resume doesn't show. Finally, it generates a phased learning path and interview questions targeted at those gaps, all exportable as Markdown. The whole interaction takes four steps. Nothing is hidden behind jargon.

Honesty is the feature

The part I'm most careful about is honesty. A resume tool that flatters you is not useful. The matching engine is a rules-based system — keywords weighted by importance, mapped to a small knowledge base of skills per track. It is explainable: every score can be traced back to a specific hit or miss in your resume. It makes no claims it can't support. This also means it runs at zero cost, works offline, and never sends your resume to a third party. For a student tool, those properties matter more than marginal accuracy.

What it took

There were lessons along the way. Deploying FastAPI to Vercel took six failed builds before it worked — the details are exactly as tedious as you imagine, but each failure taught something real about how the platform routes and packages Python functions. I wrote it down, because the next person (including me, in six months) will need it.

Where it's going

The current version is live and usable — the source code is on GitHub. The next step is semantic matching with a language model — replacing the keyword engine without changing the interface, so the tool can understand intent rather than exact words. After that: OCR for scanned resumes, accounts and history, and more job tracks.

I built this for a simple reason. When I was looking for my first job, I would have appreciated something that told me plainly what was missing. Easy-Job-Tutor is an attempt to be that, for someone else.
